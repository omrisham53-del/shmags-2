# D&D Campaign Arc: Brainstorm / Discovery Notes
Date: 2026-06-06 · Goal: Fix the few load-bearing anchors of the Dragon Riders Resurrection arc (the Betrayer, the catastrophe, the reveals, pacing, the character threads) so an improv-first DM has consistent guardrails to push against. NOT to script the campaign; outcomes stay player-determined.

## Summary / key decisions
- **DM philosophy: improv-first, light-prep.** Omri finds over-planning unfun and notices it railroads players exactly where he wants them. Wants the game free-flowing and improvisational, and wants to actively build his own improv skills by "just going for it." So the purpose of this brainstorm is NOT to script the campaign. It's to fix the few load-bearing anchors (the things that must stay consistent for a mystery/reveal to land) and stock a menu of options he can improvise from, while leaving outcomes open.
- **Ending: deliberately player-determined.** No fixed ending. Good to have a menu of ending options to pull from, but nothing pre-decided. The bounded version (Betrayer defeated either way; the live choice is the dragon's fate, crystals shattered vs seized, freedom vs control) matches his gut as one option on the menu, not the rail.
- **The Betrayer = ASHAR (anchor):** an ancient elf from the original dragon rider bloodline who has been the dragon's jailer for centuries. He's a stranger the players hunt, not a trusted NPC. Name "Ashar" = the ash of Helstone (presumed burned in the Fall, rose from it); the rider of House Arendath.
- **"Why now" (anchor, the plot engine):** the centuries-old binding is finally decaying. A forced binding is NOT a rideable pact, so to convert imprisonment into a true forced pact before the binding breaks, he needs the bloodline conversion ritual the **Scholar** knows, and he's only recently located her. This one answer explains the whole current board: why he hunts the Scholar now, why he practices forced bonding on Darklings (rehearsing the conversion), the urgency (binding is rotting), and why the players matter (they can reach the Scholar or dragon first).
- **Reveal order (loose guardrail, not level-locked):** 1) Aerendil's patron = the ancient dragon, intimate, via dreams. 2) Betrayer's identity/plan, public, flips mystery to confrontation. 3) The catastrophe, in two layers (his self-justifying version, then the truth near the climax). Each reveal reframes the last: personal -> political -> moral.
- **Character threads (each grounded, not improv-floating):** Herald = his order (already backed the Scholar's exile; arc = disillusionment -> partnership/freedom values). Aerendil = the dragon patron via the dream system. Ziggy = unwitting keeper of a suppressed song that encodes a fragment of the truth (accidental guardian of erased history; improv-friendly clue lever). The Scholar = academic who reconstructed the conversion ritual, exiled for it, now the Betrayer's missing key.
- **The truth of the catastrophe (anchor, DM-known, revealed in pieces):** **Control caused it, not freedom.** A **small faction** created a **gem** and convinced the Betrayer it would make him vastly more powerful. He tried to use it by **embedding the gem into his dragon's skin mid-air** (mid-flight) to amplify his control. Instead it caused catastrophic **loss of control**: the dragon went berserk and burned the city. The gem was **destroyed in the chaos** of the event. He buried this truth, blamed the dragon's "freedom," and built the creed "freedom failed, control is the only safe way" on top of the lie. Deepest irony: his reach for MORE control destroyed everything. The gem is **separate from the blue salt crystals** (the crystals are his later/refined method). (DM-known now; players discover it gradually.)
- **The method ladder (anchor, unifies the magic system):** One line of escalating obsession - (1) **Gem / force** -> catastrophe, taught him forcing control kills; (2) **Blue salt crystals / calm + chain** -> his desperate fallback right after the catastrophe (the ancient bloodline method), used to subdue the berserk dragon and cage it for centuries; (3) **Crystals + Scholar's ritual / calm + permanent forced pact** -> his plan now, to convert the decaying cage into a true rideable bond before it fails. The gem's makers (the small faction) are a CLOSED door, irrelevant now.
- **Why the Betrayer was never found (anchor, his ultimate camouflage):** After the catastrophe the dragon went loose and the Betrayer's body was **never recovered**. History records that he was dropped into the fire of the burning city, i.e. **he is presumed dead.** In truth he escaped and somehow subdued/captured that same loose dragon, and has kept it bound ever since. No one hunts a dead man, so for centuries no one has looked for him. This presumed-death is layered ON TOP of the orders' suppression doctrine = a double camouflage. It also definitively answers the "why hasn't he been found / why now" thread.
- **The religious orders (anchor):** They act on **sincere, independent fear**, not as anyone's puppets. They genuinely believe suppressing dragon-rider knowledge keeps the world safe (after the catastrophe). The Betrayer does NOT control them; he's a **parasite** who benefits from their doctrine and hides in its gaps. Implication: the orders are morally independent (well-meaning and wrong, not evil), Herald is leaving a sincere institution rather than unmasking a cabal, and the Scholar/Herald/Betrayer threads connect through the shared THEME of suppression/control, not literal strings. The Betrayer stays offstage via proxies (Darklings, Kobolds, possible insider) until the L4-5 reveal.
- **Pacing (anchor):** Milestone leveling tied to story beats (Omri already uses milestones). The beats are guardrails; the connective tissue between them is fully improvised. Four beats from level 2 to the level-6 climax, ~2-4 sessions each (loose, ~10-16 sessions, no fixed count):
  - **L2->3:** reach/save the Scholar **AND** Herald reaches his Oath decision (see constraint below).
  - **L3->4:** Scholar teaches the deeper history + Aerendil's patron reveal via dreams; first real scent of the Betrayer.
  - **L4->5:** Betrayer's identity + plan exposed; mystery flips to confrontation.
  - **L5->6:** party reaches the dragon / the Betrayer's seat of power for the final stand.
- **HARD CONSTRAINT on the L2->3 beat:** Herald is a Paladin, so level 3 = Oath selection (subclass). The milestone to level 3 MUST coincide with the narrative bringing Herald to his Oath decision. The level-up can't fire until that choice is earned in-story.
- **Betrayer's emotional core (anchor):** "**Freedom failed, so control is the only safe way.**" He lived through the catastrophe where a free dragon/bond destroyed a city. The world concluded "suppress dragons"; he concluded "partnership was the mistake, a dragon must be controlled, never trusted." In his own head he's not a tyrant, he's the only one brave enough to do dragon riding *safely* and prevent the next burned city. He's a tragic mirror of the party's emerging freedom/partnership values, and he's not wrong that freedom has a body count.
