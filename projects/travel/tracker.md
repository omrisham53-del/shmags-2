## Gear, Food & Trip Prep

Base advice came from a family member with real hands-on experience in the Jotunheimen/Breheimen area (captured 2026-07-22). Reviewed and gap-checked 2026-08-24 against the actual route: 11 hut nights, 4 of them DNT self-service, September at 1,400-2,000m, solo.

Items tagged `(added 08-24)` were missing from the original list. Everything else is the family member's own advice, unchanged.

**Buy-before-departure deadline: Sept 8.** Two items can only be sorted in Norway (DNT key, gas canister) and are flagged as such.

### General

- Jotunheimen is a great starting point -- recommended to hike Galdhøpiggen (Norway's highest peak) while there.
- Check weather constantly throughout the whole trip, including mid-trek -- no problem waiting a day or two in a cabin for better weather rather than pushing through.
- Weather is very unpredictable. Expect rain roughly half the days; 4-10°C on the high routes, colder at night, snow possible at altitude.
- If weather turns heavy, stay in a cabin rather than a tent.
- Tent is still the default though -- saves cost and is a real challenge Omri wants. Only fun if the weather actually allows it.
- Cabins sometimes sell extra supplies (food, firewood for heating) that make the stay and the trek nicer -- worth checking on arrival.

### Open judgment calls

**Footwear.** The family member recommended below-ankle shoes. For this specific route the common recommendation runs the other way (ankle support) because of wet terrain, river crossings and boulder fields, and the route includes the Besseggen scramble and wire-assisted sections carried with a loaded multi-day pack. Not a reason to override the advice, but worth testing the chosen pair loaded before committing.

**The 13-week problem.** This whole list is built for Norway, but it gets carried through London, Scotland, Ireland and Portugal. Scotland reuses most of it for the West Highland Way, so nothing is dead weight until early November, but the tent, stove and winter layers become baggage for the surf legs. Worth deciding now whether anything ships home after Scotland, since that changes what's worth buying.

## Norway Route: Oslo → Jotunheimen → Breheimen

Bergen dropped entirely (2026-07-18) -- decided to focus on hiking the best locations as fast as possible before the season turns. Full details below.

### Travel to Jotunheimen (Sep 8-9)

1. **Sep 8:** Land Oslo, one night in Oslo (not the same-night rush to the mountains -- deliberate, so the Sep 9 morning is free for the DNT center visit).
2. **Sep 9:** Only one daily train+bus connection reaches Gjendesheim: train Oslo→Otta (08:02–11:33), bus Otta→Vågåmo (11:50–12:20), bus Vågåmo→Gjendesheim (12:25–13:40). ~5.5h total, arriving **early afternoon, not evening** -- better than expected, leaves a few hours to settle in or start hiking that day.

### Jotunheimen -- 6 days, Sep 10-15

Route: [6 Days in Jotunheimen's Peaks](https://www.solofemalewanderer.com/blog/6-days-in-jotunheimens-peaks-english-route) -- 95.3km total, ~3,600m elevation gain.

| Day | Date | Leg | Distance | Hut (per DNT map) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sep 10 | Gjendesheim → Glitterheim | 21km | Glitterheim -- DNT staffed |
| 2 | Sep 11 | Glitterheim → Spiterstulen | 15-16km | Spiterstulen -- private staffed |
| 3 | Sep 12 | Spiterstulen → Leirvassbu | 14km | Leirvassbu -- DNT staffed |
| 4 | Sep 13 | Leirvassbu → Gjendebu | 18km | Gjendebu -- DNT staffed (last confirmed staffed day) |
| 5 | Sep 14 | Gjendebu → Memurubu (via Bukkelægeret) | 13km | Memurubu -- private staffed (⚠ see Open Questions) |
| 6 | Sep 15 | Memurubu → Gjendesheim (via Besseggen) | 14km | Exit day |

Days 5-6 include the most technical terrain (wire-assisted exposed sections, Besseggen's hands-and-knees scramble) -- fits fine for an experienced hiker, but not a beginner route.

### Transition: Jotunheimen → Breheimen (Sep 16)

Bus via Lom, then a further bridging leg to Gaupne/Høyheimsvik (Breheimen's trailhead for this route) -- likely via the Sognefjellsvegen corridor. Exact schedule unconfirmed, see Open Questions.

### Breheimen -- 5 days, Sep 17-21

Route: [From Fjord to Mountain in Breheimen](https://www.solofemalewanderer.com/blog/from-fjord-to-mountain-in-breheimen-english-route) -- 61km total, 3,000m+ elevation gain. Not recommended by the source as a first cabin-to-cabin hike, but reasonable given 6 days of Jotunheimen hut-to-hut immediately beforehand. Open to swapping for a different Breheimen route if the DNT conversation surfaces something better.

| Day | Date | Leg | Distance | Hut (per DNT map) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sep 17 | Gaupne/Høyheimsvik → Navarsete | 7.5km | Navarsete -- DNT self-service (confirmed via dnt.no, not on the map crop) |
| 2 | Sep 18 | Navarsete → Vigdalstøl | 20.4km | Vigdalstøl -- DNT self-service |
| 3 | Sep 19 | Vigdalstøl → Fast | 13.9km | Fast -- DNT self-service |
| 4 | Sep 20 | Fast → Arentzbu | 11.6km | Arentzbu -- DNT self-service |
| 5 | Sep 21 | Arentzbu → Nørdstedalseter | 15.5km | Nørdstedalseter -- DNT staffed (real meals after 4 self-catered nights) |

Exit from Nørdstedalseter: hike to Sota Sæter (also DNT staffed, confirmed on map) then shuttle to Bismo, near Lom -- convenient for the bus back to Oslo.

### After Sep 21 -- Odda / Trolltunga block (not yet locked)

Sep 22 -- ~Oct 5 (14 calendar days / ~13 usable, since ~Oct 5 itself is likely a travel/transition day toward London -- date is still an estimate, not booked).

**Transport in:** Lom/Bismo → Odda is ~9h45m by bus (via Sogndal and Voss, no direct train) -- treat as its own dedicated travel day, nothing else scheduled around it.

**Trolltunga itself:** 7-15h round trip depending on parking start point, black-graded. Needs a night in/near Odda before and after -- not a same-day hike.

**Shorter day hikes in the same area** (good for a rest day before/after Trolltunga):
- Låtefossen -- iconic twin waterfall, roadside, minimal effort
- Lilletopp -- short climb, big payoff views over Sørfjorden
- Bondhusvatnet -- easy walk to a glacial lake below Bondhusbrea
- Reinanuten -- 7.9km, tougher, Buerbreen glacier views
- Jordalsnuten -- summit hike, glacier + fjord views
- Eidesnuten -- steep sections, Sørfjorden/Sandvinsvatnet views
- Husedalen (near Kinsarvik) -- the 4-waterfall valley, already flagged earlier

**Multi-day option under consideration -- Hardangervidda crossing from Kinsarvik** (same fjord region, starts by walking up Husedalen): three length variants exist -- 3-day ("From Fjord to Mountain," Eidfjord-based), 4-day ("Best in the West"), or the full 5-6 day/109km crossing to Haukeliseter. Not chosen yet. Two things to weigh before picking one:
- Hut staffing is thin by these dates: Stavali has a caretaker July-Aug only (self-service after), Litlos is staffed July-Aug only, Torehytta/Hellevassbu are self-service/DNT-key year-round. Only Haukeliseter (the 5-6 day variant's endpoint) is staffed year-round.
- The 5-6 day variant is **one-way**, ending at Haukeliseter near the E134 road -- a separate onward transport leg from there, not a loop back to Odda. The 3-day and 4-day variants' exact endpoints aren't confirmed yet.

**Open decision:** day-hikes-only block vs. adding a 3-4 day Hardangervidda crossing -- depends on how the time budget looks once Trolltunga logistics (travel day + 2 hike/rest days minimum) are subtracted from the ~13 days available.
