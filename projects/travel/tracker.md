## Weather Watch -- decision gate Sept 5-6 (added 2026-09-01)

A family member currently in Lofoten reported persistent cloud and rain, and inferred from a wet summer that the rest of the season will stay that way. Researched it; the conclusion is **hold the plan and decide on Sept 5-6**, not now.

- **Lofoten is the wrong proxy.** It's ~900km north, maritime, and September is climatologically its wettest month (~141mm over ~24 rain days). What he's seeing is a normal Lofoten September.
- **Jotunheimen is in Norway's driest region.** Lom averages 321mm/year (Bergen ~2,250mm); published precipitation climatology puts inland areas east of the watershed below 400mm/yr, naming upper Gudbrandsdalen specifically.
- **No forecast for the trek window exists yet.** Trek runs Sept 10-21; useful model skill runs ~7-10 days. Deciding today means deciding blind.
- **"Wet summer therefore wet autumn" isn't valid.** Mid-latitude precipitation anomalies don't persist across seasons. Copernicus autumn 2026 guidance: above-average temperature is the high-confidence signal, rainfall skill is low, and the wetter lean is aimed at southern/western Europe.
- **September climatology matches what was already planned for** -- ~44% chance of precipitation per day, 7°C/1°C, ~29% clear sky. Same as the family member's own July advice. Nothing has actually changed.
- **The Sept 13 hut closure turned out to be a bigger, real issue** -- not just Memurubu, but summer bridges near Gjendebu/Spiterstulen/Glitterheim being pulled the same week, confirmed directly by DNT's 2026-09-04 email reply. Route changed again in response (see "CONFIRMED PLAN" in Norway Route below) -- Besseggen is now Day 1 (Gjendesheim→Memurubu direction), which DNT itself flagged only for fear-of-heights, not as an extra seasonal risk, and said no acclimatization is needed beyond the existing Sep 9 night at Gjendesheim.

**Gate actions:** hold the DNT membership purchase (~€60) until Sept 5-6; book nothing southward before then; on Sept 5-6 pull the real 7-day forecast for Gjendesheim/Glitterheim/Spiterstulen and chase the Memurubu Sept 14 question by email. If it's a genuine washout, the only alternatives that cover the full Sept 10 - Oct 5 window are **Picos de Europa** (real mountain terrain, season runs May-October) and **Madeira** (year-round, but day-hiking not hut-to-hut) -- every Alpine option (Dolomites, Julian Alps, Pyrenees) hits its own hut-closure wall at almost exactly the same time as Norway. Oslo escape flights are cheap (Milan/Venice/Barcelona ~€20-55 one-way).

Full research writeup with sources: artifact `Norway or South` (https://claude.ai/code/artifact/92d4a054-6a2d-46ab-91a6-f6c75a191fa9).

This is a different input from the Lofoten anecdote above: it is an actual forecast for the actual area, and at 7-18 days out the near end of it is starting to carry real skill. ## Trip Prep Notes

- Jotunheimen is a great starting point -- recommended to hike Galdhøpiggen (Norway's highest peak) while there.
- Check weather constantly throughout the whole trip, including mid-trek -- no problem waiting a day or two in a cabin for better weather rather than pushing through.
- Weather is very unpredictable. Expect rain roughly half the days; 4-10°C on the high routes, colder at night, snow possible at altitude.
- If weather turns heavy, stay in a cabin rather than a tent.
- Tent is still the default though -- saves cost and is a real challenge Omri wants. Only fun if the weather actually allows it.
- Cabins sometimes sell extra supplies (food, firewood for heating) that make the stay and the trek nicer -- worth checking on arrival.

**Meal plan**
- Breakfast -- no-cook rotation (Omri doesn't like oatmeal, skipped the family member's suggestion): tahini + halva on crackers, cream cheese + jam/honey on crackers, trail mix (nuts/dried fruit), hard cheese + crackers + dried dates/apricots, protein/energy bars for fast-start mornings. Mix of sweet and savory so it doesn't repeat lunch.
- Lunch -- tortillas/crackers with cream cheese and smoked salmon/salami/ham.
- Dinner -- small pasta (orzo/Israeli ptitim, brought from home) with an optional small soup/sauce.

### Open judgment calls

**Footwear.** The family member recommended below-ankle shoes. For this specific route the common recommendation runs the other way (ankle support) because of wet terrain, river crossings and boulder fields, and the route includes the Besseggen scramble and wire-assisted sections carried with a loaded multi-day pack. Not a reason to override the advice, but worth testing the chosen pair loaded before committing.

**The 13-week problem.** The Norway equipment list gets carried through London, Scotland, Ireland and Portugal too. Scotland reuses most of it for the West Highland Way, so nothing is dead weight until early November, but the tent, stove and winter layers become baggage for the surf legs. Worth deciding now whether anything ships home after Scotland, since that changes what's worth buying.

### After Sep 21 -- Odda / Trolltunga block, still wanted but weather-dependent (confirmed 2026-09-09)

**Breheimen shortened at the Oslo DNT center (2026-09-09, area transport closing down for the season) -- exit is now Skjolden, not Lom/Sota Sæter.** See `norway-route.md`. Omri confirmed he's still chasing Odda/Trolltunga from here, but is flexible on it depending on weather -- Skjolden → Oslo direct is the fallback if conditions don't cooperate.

Sep 22 -- ~Oct 6 (estimate, not booked).

**Transport in:** Skjolden → Odda, via Sogndal and Voss (aggregator estimate, ~8h incl. transfers) -- **not independently verified against a real operator yet**, same caution as every other aggregator-sourced leg on this trip that turned out wrong or stale (see the Lom-side bus corridor and the Otta bus, both resolved only by checking the operator/local source directly). Check Entur (entur.no, Norway's national journey planner) or a local source before relying on it, ideally while still at Fast/Vigdalstøl with signal.

**Trolltunga itself:** 7-15h round trip depending on parking start point, black-graded. Needs a night in/near Odda before and after -- not a same-day hike.

**Shorter day hikes in the same area** (good for a rest day before/after Trolltunga):
- Låtefossen -- iconic twin waterfall, roadside, minimal effort
- Lilletopp -- short climb, big payoff views over Sørfjorden
- Bondhusvatnet -- easy walk to a glacial lake below Bondhusbrea
- Reinanuten -- 7.9km, tougher, Buerbreen glacier views
- Jordalsnuten -- summit hike, glacier + fjord views
- Eidesnuten -- steep sections, Sørfjorden/Sandvinsvatnet views
- Husedalen (near Kinsarvik) -- the 4-waterfall valley, already flagged earlier

**Multi-day option under consideration -- Hardangervidda crossing from Kinsarvik** (same fjord region, starts by walking up Husedalen): three length variants exist -- 3-day ("From Fjord to Mountain," Eidfjord-based), 4-day ("Best in the West"), or the full 5-6 day/109km crossing to Haukeliseter. Not chosen yet. Two things to weigh before picking one:
- Hut staffing is thin by these dates: Stavali has a caretaker July-Aug only (self-service after), Litlos is staffed July-Aug only, Torehytta/Hellevassbu are self-service/DNT-key year-round. Only Haukeliseter (the 5-6 day variant's endpoint) is staffed year-round.
- The 5-6 day variant is **one-way**, ending at Haukeliseter near the E134 road -- a separate onward transport leg from there, not a loop back to Odda. The 3-day and 4-day variants' exact endpoints aren't confirmed yet.

**Open decision:** day-hikes-only block vs. adding a 3-4 day Hardangervidda crossing -- depends on how the time budget looks once Trolltunga logistics (travel day + 2 hike/rest days minimum) are subtracted from the ~13 days available.
