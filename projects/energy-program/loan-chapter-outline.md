## Trim strategy, in order of highest-value cuts first

Cutting a full-content 6-page document to half its length by trimming sentences everywhere
usually reads worse than cutting whole low-value chunks and tightening what's left. Work in
this order:

1. **Cut redundant explanation, not unique content.** Anything the position paper explains that
   the grants or tax chapter already establishes for the reader (e.g. the shared policy
   background of Government Decision 1261, if repeated here) can be cut to a one-line reference
   instead of re-explained.
2. **Cut or compress examples/illustrations that aren't load-bearing.** If the position paper
   has a worked numerical example or an extended case for how a firm would use the fund, keep
   the number, cut the narrative walk-through.
3. **Compress any international comparison to a table, if not already one.** Same move the tax
   chapter's international review used -- prose comparisons run long; a table with the same
   parameters is much denser per page.
4. **Tighten background/context prose.** This is usually where a position paper (written to
   stand alone, persuade a reader with no other context) carries the most fat once it's one
   chapter among three siblings the reader has already read. Cut scene-setting, keep the actual
   policy mechanism and numbers.
5. **Last resort: cut detail from the mechanism/methodology sections themselves.** Only after
   1-4 are exhausted -- these are the sections that actually carry the chapter's substance.

## What NOT to cut

- The core mechanism explanation (how the loan fund actually works -- terms, eligibility).
- Any real numbers/results already in the position paper.
- Anything that makes this chapter's role among the 3 instruments (grants = cash outlay, tax =
  timing benefit, loan fund = access to financing) clear -- a reader hitting this as the third
  of three chapters needs that framing to land, briefly.
