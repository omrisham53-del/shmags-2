# Life Cycle Assessment: Dairy Milk vs. Soy Milk

## Introduction

Life Cycle Assessment (LCA) is a standardized methodology for quantifying the environmental impacts of a product across its entire existence, from raw material extraction to end-of-life disposal. This assignment applies LCA principles following ISO 14040/14044 standards to compare two competing products that serve the same consumer function: conventional dairy milk and soy milk. The comparison reveals substantial environmental differences concentrated at distinct lifecycle stages for each product, with water use and agricultural inputs emerging as the most critical divergence points.

The functional unit for this analysis is one liter of refrigerated milk delivered to the consumer. This unit enables a fair comparison between two production systems that differ fundamentally in their input requirements, processing methods, and supply chains.

---

## System Boundary

The system boundary encompasses six lifecycle stages for both products:

1. **Raw Material Extraction** - feed crops, soybeans, water inputs, and energy at the agricultural stage
2. **Material Processing and Manufacturing** - pasteurization and refrigeration for dairy; soaking, grinding, boiling, and filtration for soy milk
3. **Assembly, Packaging, and Preparation** - carton construction (paper, plastic, aluminum laminates) for both products
4. **Distribution and Retail Delivery** - cold chain logistics and refrigerated retail storage
5. **Use Phase** - home refrigeration over a 2-3 week shelf life
6. **End-of-Life** - carton recycling and wastewater treatment

This cradle-to-grave boundary captures all material and energy flows relevant to the functional unit. Both products use comparable packaging and distribution infrastructure, making within-system differences attributable primarily to agricultural and processing stages.

## Methodology

This LCA follows the ISO 14040/14044 framework, covering goal and scope definition, inventory analysis, impact assessment, and interpretation. Environmental impact categories examined include water consumption, greenhouse gas emissions, land use, and eutrophication potential. Data is drawn from peer-reviewed LCA studies and meta-analyses covering multiple regional production systems.

## Results and Analysis

### Water Footprint

Water consumption is the most striking difference between the two systems. Dairy milk requires 628 liters of water per liter of milk produced, driven primarily by the water embedded in feed crop cultivation for cattle (Poore & Nemecek, 2018). Soy milk requires 27-28 liters per liter of final product, approximately 22 times less water (Poore & Nemecek, 2018). This difference reflects the inefficiency of converting plant calories through an animal intermediary: cattle consume large volumes of water-intensive feed to produce a relatively small volume of milk.

### Environmental Hotspots

For dairy milk, raw milk production dominates the environmental profile, accounting for 75-85% of total lifecycle impacts (Gonzalez-Garcia et al., 2025). The main drivers within this hotspot are:

- Feed crop production (land use, fertilizer inputs, pesticides)
- Enteric fermentation (methane emissions from cattle digestion)
- On-farm energy consumption
- Manure management and associated nitrous oxide emissions

For soy milk, the hotspot profile shifts. Raw material extraction is relatively benign since soybeans are nitrogen-fixing legumes that require lower fertilizer inputs than feed grains. The significant impacts concentrate instead at processing, packaging, and transportation stages (Gonzalez-Garcia et al., 2025). This makes soy milk's impact profile more amenable to improvement through industrial efficiency measures, whereas dairy milk's dominant hotspot -- livestock metabolism -- is structurally harder to address.

### Processing Methods

Processing differences between the two products are substantial and affect both energy demand and the environmental footprint of the manufacturing stage.

**Dairy milk processing:**
- Pasteurization: thermal treatment to pathogen-safe temperatures
- Continuous cooling to maintain product safety
- Ongoing refrigeration throughout the supply chain and use phase
- Energy intensity is high due to the unbroken cold chain requirement

**Soy milk processing:**
- Dried soybeans are soaked in water
- Mechanical grinding produces an aqueous slurry
- Boiling for 15-20 minutes inactivates soy trypsin inhibitor (STI), a naturally occurring anti-nutritional compound (Cao et al., 2009)
- Straining and filtration removes soy pulp (okara)
- Defoaming agents (glycerin-based fatty acid esters) are added during the boiling stage
- The product still requires refrigeration after opening, but overall cold chain requirements are less intensive than dairy

### Lifecycle Stage Comparison

The six lifecycle stages distribute environmental burden differently across the two products:

- **Stage 1 (Raw materials):** Dairy carries much higher burden due to cattle feed production and water inputs. Soy carries lower burden due to legume nitrogen fixation and simpler agronomic requirements.
- **Stage 2 (Processing):** Both require energy-intensive thermal treatment. Dairy's continuous refrigeration requirement exceeds soy's batch boiling step.
- **Stage 3 (Packaging):** Comparable between products; both use composite carton packaging.
- **Stage 4 (Distribution):** Dairy requires unbroken cold chain. Some soy milk formats (UHT/aseptic) allow ambient distribution, reducing energy burden at this stage.
- **Stage 5 (Use phase):** Both require household refrigeration after opening, creating comparable impacts.
- **Stage 6 (End-of-life):** Both generate composite carton waste with similar recycling challenges.

## Discussion

The LCA comparison demonstrates a consistent pattern: dairy milk's environmental impacts are dominated by an inherently inefficient biological conversion process, while soy milk's impacts are concentrated at industrial stages more amenable to technological improvement. The 22-fold water use difference is the clearest quantitative signal of this structural disparity (Poore & Nemecek, 2018).

The systematic review by Gonzalez-Garcia et al. (2025), covering 84 LCA studies, confirms that this hotspot pattern holds across diverse production regions and methodological variations -- making it a robust finding rather than an artifact of any single study's assumptions.

From a policy and consumer choice standpoint, the LCA evidence suggests that shifting from dairy to plant-based alternatives offers meaningful environmental benefits, particularly for water-stressed regions. ## Conclusions and Recommendations

The LCA comparison of dairy milk and soy milk per one-liter functional unit yields three main conclusions:

- Water consumption strongly favors soy milk: 27-28 L/liter versus 628 L/liter for dairy, a 22-fold difference driven by cattle feed requirements (Poore & Nemecek, 2018).
- Environmental hotspots differ structurally: dairy's dominant impacts occur at the raw milk production stage (75-85% of total impacts), while soy milk's impacts concentrate at processing and packaging (Gonzalez-Garcia et al., 2025).
- Processing methods diverge in energy profile: dairy requires continuous refrigeration across its entire supply chain; soy requires a defined thermal treatment for STI inactivation (Cao et al., 2009) but has more flexibility in distribution temperature.

For decision-makers concerned with water use and agricultural greenhouse gas emissions, soy milk presents a substantially lower environmental burden per liter delivered. Efforts to further reduce soy milk's footprint should target packaging materials and processing energy efficiency, as these are the product's primary impact hotspots.
