### A1. מבקר עיריית הרצליה (2021). דוח ביקורת בנושא מניעת זיהום חופים וים. עיריית הרצליה.
https://www.herzliya.muni.il/uploads/n/1690718095.2456.pdf
**Tier A — the single most important source in the paper.** 36 pages. This is the city's own auditor confirming the exact gap the project addresses. Already cited in the group's lit review, but only shallowly; the real content is far stronger than what was used.

### A2. המשרד להגנת הסביבה. עקרונות והנחיות לתכנון נקזים עירוניים עם מוצא בחוף.
Referenced extensively inside A1 (section 5.2). **Tier A.** Need to locate the standalone document; currently citable second-hand via the auditor report.

### A3. החוק למניעת זיהום הים ממקורות יבשתיים, התשמ"ח-1988 (ותקנותיו).
In force 1990. Basis: Barcelona Convention. **Tier A** — the regulatory anchor.

### A4. מנהל התכנון / אגמא — מרכז הידע לניהול נגר עילי עירוני. מדיניות ניהול הנגר.
https://runoff.agma.org.il/ (© 2022)
**Tier A** — national urban runoff policy: runoff management integrated at four planning tiers (regional, municipal, neighbourhood, parcel), quantitative volumetric targets, a runoff calculator (מחשבון הנגר), and a toolkit that explicitly includes bioretention and filtration pits.

### A5. המשרד להגנת הסביבה — תוכנית "חוף נקי" / קול קורא לתמיכה ברשויות חופיות.
~₪9.7M per year (Nov 2024 – Oct 2025) for coastal authorities. Budget explicitly designated for, among other things, **facilities preventing stormwater runoff waste from reaching the sea**, covering **166 drainage points** along **153 km** of open beach.
Reported: https://www.be106.net/49/122595 ; https://infospot.co.il/n/environmental_challenges
**Tier A for the business model** — this is the funding mechanism. Verify the current-year call before submission.

### B1. הרות, ב., ושפר, ע. (2014). מה נשתנה? איכות מימי החופין של ישראל בים התיכון, משנות ה-80 ועד היום. אקולוגיה וסביבה, 5(1), 16–24.
https://magazine.isees.org.il/?p=15802
**Tier B** — peer-reviewed Israeli journal. Point sources dropped sharply since 2000 (~90% reduction in heavy metals/fertilisers to Kishon and coastal streams; cadmium −80% 2004–2012), which makes **diffuse sources such as runoff the relatively growing share** of the remaining problem. Explicitly notes diffuse sources are significant but poorly quantified. This is the strongest available framing for why runoff matters *now*.

### B2. קק"ל — ביופילטר כפר סבא (מרכז פרויקטי מו"פ).
https://www.kkl.org.il/research_and_development_projects/projects-center/kfar-saba-biofilter.aspx
https://www.kkl.org.il/water_activity_urban_run_off_kfar_saba_biofilter/
**Tier B** — the Israeli case study. First biofilter in Israel, installed 2010–2011. Treats 4,000–7,000 m³/yr from roughly one third of the municipal urban area. Dual function: treats runoff in winter, polishes nitrate-contaminated well water in dry season. Partner: Friends of KKL Australia. Note: the group's current lit review cites the KKL *blog* (2015); cite this R&D page instead, it is more authoritative.

### B3. המרכז לערים רגישות מים בישראל (WSC Israel) — פרויקט 3.1: פיתוח ביופילטר היברידי לטיפול במי נגר עירוני ולשיקום מי תהום מזוהמים בחנקות.
wsc.org.il — **site was DNS-unreachable on 08/08/2026, retry before submission.** Known from search indexing to host the Kfar Saba hybrid biofilter/rain garden project page and a position paper (נייר עמדה).
**Tier B, potentially Tier A** — an Israeli water-sensitive-cities research center is both a citable source and a named collaboration partner. Worth a retry; if still down, cite via KKL + Water Authority instead.

### B4. ורצברגר, ר. (2004). מסמך רקע בנושא: מפגעים סביבתיים בחופי הרחצה. מרכז המחקר והמידע של הכנסת.
https://fs.knesset.gov.il/globaldocs/MMM/b0b7af38-9732-e811-80de-00155d0a0235/2_b0b7af38-9732-e811-80de-00155d0a0235_11_9195.pdf
**Tier C — 22 years old.** Use only for the regulatory/institutional framework, never for current data. Israel's coastline ~280 km, of which only 127 declared bathing beaches totalling ~18.8 km at the time.

### B5. בן מיכאל, ר. (2018, 16 באוגוסט). כמה שווה לך חוף נקי? זווית — סוכנות ידיעות למדע ולסביבה.
https://www.zavit.org.il/כמה-שווה-לך-חוף-נקי/
**Tier B** — reports joint valuation work by the Center for Natural Resources and Environmental Research (University of Haifa), the Israel Oceanographic and Limnological Research institute, and the Kinneret Laboratory. Preserving extensive coastal marine areas undeveloped valued at **up to ₪2.3 billion/year**. Also relays a US Orange County finding that cutting beach pollution 75% would be worth **$186M/yr (~₪651M)** in beach tourism value alone.
NOTE: this is a science-journalism secondary source. Trace to the underlying academic report before leaning on the ₪2.3B figure in the paper.

### B6. מבקר עיריית תל אביב-יפו (2023). דוח ביקורת בנושא מערכת הניקוז העירונית.

## English Sources

### A6. Drapper, D., & Hornbuckle, A. (2018). Removal of nutrients, sediment, and heavy metals by a stormwater treatment train; a medium-density residential case study in Southeast Queensland. *Water, 10*(10), 1307.
https://www.mdpi.com/2073-4441/10/10/1307
**Tier A** — already the group's anchor case study. Peer-reviewed, 4-year field monitoring. Media filter cartridges (perlite, zeolite, activated alumina), 85 cm. Removal: ~80% TSS, ~60% phosphorus, ~50% heavy metals. No breakthrough after 4+ years without cartridge replacement; maintenance limited mainly to sediment removal from the sub-surface vault.

### A7. U.S. EPA (2015). *LID stormwater control cost estimation analysis*. Office of Research and Development. EP-C-11-036 TO-19.
https://pasteur.epa.gov/uploads/10.23719/1510483/documents/LID%20Cost%20Analysis_Report_National%20SWC_2015.pdf
**Tier A for the economic model.** 72 pages. Key figures extracted:
- MPCA (2011), capital cost per ft³ of water-quality volume: **bioretention basins $15**, **biofiltration basins $58**, underground infiltration $213, infiltration trenches $11, pervious pavement $16
- MPCA (2011) annual maintenance: bioretention **$1.25/ft³/yr** (≈8% of capital)
- Unit costs per ft³ effective treatment volume (2011$), bioretention basin: simple $3.00, typical $8.00, complex $46.00, **redevelopment/retrofit $11.00** ← the relevant case for Herzliya
- SBPAT (2005$) per acre treated: bioretention $23,100; manufactured separation systems $44,700; media filter maintenance $1,500/acre/yr
- Curb-contained bioretention per acre of drainage area: no underdrain avg $42,254; with underdrain avg $89,000
- Rain gardens: $3/ft² (simple) to $40/ft² (complex)

### B7. Payne, E. G. I., Pham, T., Deletic, A., Hatt, B. E., Cook, P. L. M., & Fletcher, T. D. (2018). Which species? A decision-support tool to guide plant selection in stormwater biofilters. *Advances in Water Resources*.
Plus: Payne et al. (2014), Processes and drivers of nitrogen removal in stormwater biofiltration; Payne et al. (2013), Stormwater biofiltration: the challenges of inorganic and organic nitrogen removal.
https://research.monash.edu/
**Tier B** — Monash/FAWB is the scientific origin of the biofilter technology the Israeli installations are based on. 220-column experiment across 20 Australian native species. Establishes that **plant species selection and a saturated zone materially determine nitrogen removal** — directly relevant to adapting the design to Israeli/Mediterranean species, which is one of the project's genuine differentiators.

### B8. Hatt, B. E., Fletcher, T. D., & Deletic, A. (2009). Hydrologic and pollutant removal performance of stormwater biofiltration systems at the field scale. *Journal of Hydrology*.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0022169408005969
**Tier B** — field-scale (not lab) performance. ~33% of inflow volume retained on average; substantial reduction in total runoff volumes and flow peaks.

### B9. A review on microbial contaminants in stormwater runoff and outfalls: potential health risks and mitigation strategies. *Science of the Total Environment* (PMC7126443).
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7126443/
**Tier B** — the health-risk link. Stormwater outfalls deliver bacteria and viruses including human pathogens to coastal water.

### B10. First flush stormwater pollution in urban catchments: a review of its characterization and quantification towards optimization of control measures. *Journal of Environmental Management* (2023).
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0301479723007648
**Tier B** — the "הגשם הראשון" / first-flush phenomenon, which is the mechanism the entire project rests on. Essential: the group asserts first flush repeatedly but has never cited a source for it.

### B11. Pollutants in urban runoff: scientific evidence on toxicity and impacts on freshwater ecosystems (2024). *Science of the Total Environment*.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0045653524027073
**Tier B** — ecotoxicological risk of urban runoff highest for metals, PAHs, pesticides; copper, zinc, cadmium, anthracene, fluoranthene specifically.

### C1. Contech Engineered Solutions — Jellyfish Filter / StormFilter.
https://www.conteches.com/stormwater-management/filtration/jellyfish-filter/
**Tier C, competitor evidence only** — commercial manufactured treatment device (MTD). Membrane filtration, compact, manhole/vault configurations. Hundreds of standalone BMP regulatory approvals in the US.

### C2. Ayala Water & Ecology Ltd.
https://ayala-aqua.com/
**Tier C, competitor/partner evidence only** — Israeli company, founded 2002, Zipori, CEO Eli Cohen. "Natural Biological System" (NBS) phytoremediation: aquatic plants, gravel, microorganisms. Claims negligible O&M, zero energy and chemicals. Explicitly markets stormwater harvesting and drainage. **The most direct Israeli competitor, and simultaneously the most plausible commercial partner.**
